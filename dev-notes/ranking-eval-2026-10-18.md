# Ranking eval: which ranking should impact / review / pack / find use? (2026-10-18)

James, 2026-10-18: *"we should just quickly evaluate these options right now and various hybrid models immediately and as
quickly as you can probably agentically"*. Then, on the learnings behind it: *"i can almost feel like the shape of something
like person is missing but i see the shadow of an idea or learning"*, refined to *"structure with relevancy and
understanding"*.

glia's answers rank results by Personalised PageRank (PPR, damping 0.5, `glia_activation`), adopted on HippoRAG's
multi-path argument and never measured against a baseline. This run compares, on three repos, PPR against:
- BFS depth;
- popularity;
- co-change history;
- a past-task "intent" signal;
- cluster pooling;
- early fusion (relevance inside the walk);
- late fusion (relevance blended into the ranking).

The harness is `engine/examples/rank_eval.rs` (Rust, glia-engine's own dependencies, git via `std::process`).

## Bottom line

- **Does PPR beat BFS? Only on the big repo.**
  - On glia, P1 (today's PPR) beats B0 (BFS hop depth over the carry edges) by +0.039 recall@10, CI [+0.021, +0.057],
    and by +0.060 MRR.
  - On Kina, B0 is nominally ahead: P1 - B0 = -0.017 [-0.038, +0.004].
  - On quokka-stack the difference is +0.021 [-0.074, +0.130].
  - Neither of the last two is significant. The multi-path argument buys at most about 4 points of recall@10 at file level.
- **Co-change alone (C0) does not win.**
  - It is the weakest real signal in every repo: recall@10 0.163 / 0.321 / 0.275 on glia / Kina / quokka, against P1's
    0.294 / 0.426 / 0.615.
  - It fails cold: 37-49% of seeds have no earlier commit.
  - As a second key behind PPR it helps. H10 (co-change, then P1) gains over P1:

    | repo | recall@10 | MRR |
    |---|---|---|
    | glia | +0.027 | +0.095 |
    | Kina | +0.053 | +0.104 |
    | quokka | +0.021 (not significant) | +0.144 |

- **Intent plus past work beats structure, by a lot.**
  - T1 ranks the files touched by the 20 past commits whose messages best match (BM25) the test commit's own message.
  - T1 alone beats P1 on glia (+0.031, CI just includes 0) and on Kina (+0.051). It loses on quokka (-0.205; only 82
    training commits).
  - The random-message control T0 scores far lower, so the gain is intent, not popularity:

    | repo | T0 recall@10 | T1 recall@10 |
    |---|---|---|
    | glia | 0.025 | 0.324 |
    | Kina | 0.272 | 0.477 |
    | quokka | 0.291 | 0.410 |

  - The best family everywhere is H9 = a*minmax(T1) + (1-a)*minmax(PPR). With today's P1 as the base (fixed, not selected
    on test):

    | repo | H9a25[P1] recall@10 | H9a50[P1] recall@10 | gain over P1 |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | glia | 0.411 | 0.404 | +0.117 [+0.096, +0.138] / +0.110 |
    | Kina | 0.508 | 0.531 | +0.082 / +0.106 |
    | quokka | 0.673 | 0.625 | +0.058 [-0.009, +0.126], not significant |

    On glia that is +40% relative recall@10 over today's ranking, and +0.156 over BFS.
- **Early fusion is no better than late fusion.**
  - U1 reweights each edge by its endpoint files' prior co-change support. Its recall@10 equals P1's (+0.005 glia, +0.011
    Kina, +0.038 quokka), but its MRR rises everywhere: +0.025 / +0.052 / +0.080, each CI above 0.
  - U2, as specified, puts the top-10 T1 files into the restart set at a fixed 0.25. It raises recall@20 but drops recall@5
    and MRR below P1: the T1 files take the top slots in a flat order.
  - U3 (post-hoc: restart weight proportional to the T1 score) is the best early fusion: glia 0.384, Kina 0.533, quokka
    0.567. It still trails H9 on glia (0.411) and quokka (0.673), and ties it on Kina (0.531).
  - Why: most co-changing file pairs share no direct edge. CALLS joins 7-14% of them and IMPORTS 8-16%.
- **Structural details.**
  - Sum pooling of node scores (P1sum) beats max pooling in all three repos: +0.012 to +0.016 recall@10 (CI above 0 on glia
    and Kina), +0.034 to +0.049 MRR (CI above 0 everywhere).
  - Service pooling (K1svc) helps on glia (+0.020 recall@10, +0.083 MRR) and is neutral elsewhere.
  - Directory, top-2-segment and Leiden pooling lower recall@10 everywhere.
  - Forward-only and backward-only PPR are far worse than undirected.
  - The repair / review / onboard presets sit within +-0.01 recall@10 of the base weights. The exception is onboard on
    quokka (+0.060 [+0.022, +0.111], with lower MRR).
  - Idf hub damping (P2) helps on glia (+0.015 recall@10, +0.059 MRR) but significantly hurts Kina (-0.054 recall@10), so
    it is not a default.
  - Push PPR (P3) ranks like P1, within +0.006.

## Method

**Repos.**
- Kina 8ac238c: Go, Angular TS, Solidity.
- quokka-stack a77d4cb: Go, Angular TS, Flutter Dart. The committed `tools/ui-state-map/editor/node_modules` is skipped by
  glia's walk.
- glia 196728e3 (`0.5.2-complete`): Rust plus about 1,700 non-Rust files, mostly multi-language bench and test fixtures.

**How the graphs and history were read.**
- Graphs were built in memory with `glia_engine::generate_one` on `git archive` copies under `~/.cache/glia-rank-eval/`
  (`GLIA_NO_PERSIST=1`); nothing was persisted.
- History came from `git -C <live repo> log --topo-order --no-merges --no-renames --name-only <snapshot>`. The live
  checkouts were not written to.

| repo | nodes | edges | candidate files | non-merge commits | training commits | test commits | queries | mean targets | mean seed nodes | chance recall@10 | seeds without co-change history |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kina | 5909 | 15727 | 543 | 291 | 239 | 199 | 764 | 4.65 | 20.6 | 0.0185 | 255 (33%) |
| quokka-stack | 3414 | 7878 | 206 | 137 | 82 | 40 | 110 | 3.16 | 22.4 | 0.0488 | 54 (49%) |
| glia | 36424 | 124333 | 2549 | 1176 | 957 | 300 | 1253 | 5.95 | 61.2 | 0.0039 | 588 (47%) |

**Dataset.**
- **Candidates:** files with a MODULE node in the snapshot graph, minus:
  - docs, data, lockfiles and manifests (an extension deny-list);
  - files whose MODULE carries ORIGIN `generated`.
- **Test commits:** the most recent up to 300 non-merge commits with 2..=30 candidate files.
- **Queries:** each test commit gives up to 5 seed files, in a deterministic hash order of (sha, path).
  - Seed nodes are every node `Locator` places in the file, its MODULE included.
  - Targets are the commit's other candidate files.
  - Candidates are every candidate file except the seed.
- **File score:** max over the file's nodes (sum = P1sum). Ties go to the smaller path.

**Leakage.**
- Oldest-to-newest replay. Co-change counts (C0), edge co-change support (U*) and the BM25 past-task corpus (T*) for a
  test commit use only commits strictly older than it.
- Training uses non-merge commits with 1..=30 candidate files.
- The graph is the snapshot's, so structure added later is visible to every structural ranker alike (reported, not fixed).

**Rankers.**

| id | definition |
|---|---|
| B0 | BFS depth from the seeds over `CODE_TABLES.carry_edges`, undirected (`algo::reach::bfs`), then file degree descending; unreached last |
| B0i | B0 plus IMPORTS |
| B1 | file degree (popularity control) |
| B2 | path hash (chance control) |
| B3 | shared leading directory segments with the seed, then degree |
| C0 | c(seed, f) / (c(seed) + 1) |
| P1 | `CODE_TABLES.activation_config(None)`, Undirected, top_k all, over every edge (bit-identical to `ActivationPlan::run` on 40 queries per repo) |
| P1sum | P1, file score = sum over the file's nodes |
| P1f / P1b | P1 Forward / Backward |
| P1repair / P1review / P1onboard | P1 with that preset's weights |
| P2 / P2i | P1 with `Specificity::Idf` / `InverseIdf` |
| P3 | push PPR (`algo::ppr`, alpha 0.5, epsilon 1e-6 per seed node), averaged over seed nodes |
| K1x / K2x | cluster pooling of P1 / P2: cluster score = sum of member file scores; x = dir, top2, leiden, svc |
| H1aA / H1rA | A*norm(P1) + (1-A)*norm(C0), A in {.25, .5, .75}; a = min-max, r = rank normalisation |
| H2aA | as H1a with P2 |
| H3 | RRF(P1, C0), k = 60 |
| H4 | carry-BFS depth <= 3 first, by (C0, P1); then the rest by P1 |
| H5[K] | RRF(K, C0) |
| T1 | past tasks = older commits. Message: trailers dropped, lowercased, split on non-alphanumerics, stop-words and 1-2-char tokens dropped. Query = the test commit's own message. BM25 k1 1.2, b 0.75. Top 20 tasks; file score = sum of their scores over the files they touched |
| T2 | T1 over past tasks that touched the seed |
| T0 | control: T1 with the query replaced by a deterministic random older message |
| H6 / H7 / H8 | RRF(T1, P1) / RRF(T1, C0) / RRF(T1, C0, P1) |
| H9aA[S] | A*minmax(T1) + (1-A)*norm(S) for every structural S |
| U1bB | edge weight = base * (1 + B*s(e)), B in {1, 5}; s(e) = older commits touching both endpoint files / touching either |
| U2bB / U2fbB | U1 plus the top-10 T1 files in the restart set at 0.25 (two readings) |
| H10-H18, U3 (post-hoc) | H10 (C0, P1) lexicographic; H11 (T1, C0, P1); H12 RRF(T2, C0, P1); H13-H17 RRF(T1, C0, X); H18 (mm T1 + mm C0 + mm P1)/3; U3 = U1b5 with restart weight 0.25*t1(f)/max t1 |

**Metrics.** recall@5, recall@10, recall@20, MRR and nDCG@20 (binary relevance), averaged over queries.

**Paired tests.** Each ranker minus B0, P1 and C0, per query:
- the mean difference;
- a 95% commit-cluster bootstrap CI (1,000 resamples of test commits): this is the primary test;
- an exact two-sided sign test over queries.

## Conclusion

1. **PPR vs BFS.** PPR is a small win on the large repo and a tie on two application repos, never a significant loss. Keep
   it, but give it little credit: every signal from outside the graph moves the ranking more, including co-change used only
   as a tie-break.
2. **Co-change.** Alone it loses (cold starts, coarse counts). As P1's second key (H10) or blended at 0.25 (H1a25) it adds
   about +0.10 MRR in all three repos.
3. **Intent plus past work beats structure.**
   - +0.12 recall@10 on glia (0.294 -> 0.411, +40%; +0.16 over BFS, +0.25 over C0), +0.08 to +0.11 on Kina, +0.06 (not
     significant) on quokka.
   - The T0 control shows the gain is intent, not popularity.
   - The fusion must keep magnitudes: the min-max blend H9 beats RRF (H6 / H8).
   - It must keep structure: T1 alone is +0.03 over P1 on glia, H9 is +0.12.
4. **Early fusion.** As specified it does not beat late fusion. U1 sharpens the top but not recall; U3 ties late fusion on
   Kina and trails it elsewhere.
   - At file granularity, "relevance shaping the structure" is not a gain, because the relevant files mostly have no direct
     edge to the seed.
   - That itself is the inefficiency the map shows: 84-93% of the file pairs that change together share no direct edge. The
     code graph lacks the connective tissue the work follows (features, tasks, the "why").

**Recommendations per answer type:**
- **review** (change seeds plus a PR description or commit message): H9a50[P1] = 0.5*minmax(T1 over the PR text) +
  0.5*minmax(P1), with sum pooling to files. An optional C0 term (H18) has similar recall and MRR 0.581 vs 0.570 on glia.
  With no text, fall back to the impact ranking.
- **impact / blast-radius** (change seeds, no text): P1 (undirected, base weights) with sum pooling, then co-change as the
  second key (H10), or H1a25. Without history, P1sum. Idf damping and service pooling: not by default, but worth it on
  large multi-project repos like glia.
- **find** (text-resolved seeds; single-file seeds were a generous stand-in): the query text is the intent, so use the H9
  blend of BM25-over-past-commits and P1. Without history, P1sum.
- **pack** (budgeted: judge by MRR / recall@5): H9a50[P1] or H18 when text exists; H10 without it (+0.10 MRR over P1).
  Keep P1's index hot between calls.

## Caveats a skeptic should raise

1. **The target is co-editing, not breakage**, and the history-trained rankers are scored on the same kind of event they
   train on.
2. **T1's query is the commit's own message**, written after the change and often naming modules (444 `.rs`-path-like
   lines in glia's last 300 messages). It is an optimistic stand-in for a PR or issue text.
3. **History cut-offs.** The snapshot graph is used for old commits; files deleted before the snapshot drop out;
   `--no-renames` cuts renamed files' older history; commits over 30 code files are excluded.
4. **Whole-file seeds** (mean 21-61 nodes); production seeds are symbols or text matches.
5. **Multiple comparisons.** 132 rankers on one test set; best-base and top-8 rows are selected on test; H10-H18 and U3 are
   post-hoc; the fixed-base H9[P1] still has its a picked from three.
6. **The sign test** assumes independent queries and ignores magnitudes; the commit-cluster bootstrap is primary.
7. **Sample sizes.** quokka is small; Kina's history is short; glia's history is agent-written waves, with fixtures
   dominating the cross-stack categories.
8. **U2's 0.25 is ambiguous**; both readings were run.
9. **Lift vs random pairs** overstates every category (locality); only the within-repo order informs.
